# Methodology & Decision Log

This document records major analytical, methodological, and scope decisions made throughout the capstone project.

The purpose of this log is to preserve the reasoning behind the project as it develops and provide a transparent record of changes to the research approach.

---

## September 20, 2026 — Case Study Track Selection

**Decision:** Selected Track B — choose an original research question and public datasets.

**Reasoning:**  
The project is intended to demonstrate independent problem definition, dataset selection, analytical decision-making, and communication rather than completion of a predefined case study.

---

## September 20, 2026 — Initial Project Concept

**Decision:** Explore whether an integrated community technology initiative could address several related problems:

- Digital access inequality
- Digital-literacy and technical-support gaps
- Youth technical education
- Older-adult technology support
- Fraud vulnerability
- Device repair and reuse
- Electronic-waste reduction

**Reasoning:**  
These issues may share common underlying resources and interventions. A community technology program could potentially combine donated hardware, repair training, device redistribution, and technical education rather than treating each problem independently.

---

## September 20, 2026 — Scope Control

**Decision:** The project will not assume that every proposed benefit is established or that a future program would automatically produce positive outcomes.

**Analytical approach:**  
The capstone will separately evaluate:

1. Whether measurable need exists
2. Which populations appear most affected
3. Whether usable technology can realistically be recovered through repair
4. Whether comparable interventions have demonstrated feasibility
5. Which outcomes would still require testing through a future pilot program

**Reasoning:**  
Separating these questions reduces the risk of beginning with a desired conclusion and searching only for evidence that supports it.

---

## September 20, 2026 — Working Business Task

**Decision:** Use the following working business task:

> Determine whether available public evidence supports investment in an integrated community technology initiative that refurbishes donated devices, provides technical and repair training for youth, expands access to computing resources, and provides digital-literacy and technical-support services to underserved populations.

The analysis should identify which needs appear strongest, which program components are most strongly supported by evidence, and which outcomes would require further testing.

---

## September 20, 2026 — Primary Data Strategy

**Decision:** Begin with two primary analytical datasets:

### P01 — NTIA Internet Use Survey

Primary purpose:

- Analyze digital access
- Analyze device use
- Examine demographic differences
- Identify populations experiencing potential access or participation gaps

### P02 — Open Repair Alliance Data

Primary purpose:

- Analyze repair outcomes
- Compare repairability by product category
- Examine product age
- Identify common barriers preventing successful repair

**Reasoning:**  
Together, these datasets address two central questions:

> Who appears to experience technology-access barriers?

and

> To what extent can existing technology be returned to useful service?

---

## September 20, 2026 — Supporting Evidence Strategy

**Decision:** Use additional government, academic, and institutional sources primarily for context and validation rather than treating every source as a primary analytical dataset.

Supporting domains include:

- Digital skills
- Youth device access
- Older-adult fraud vulnerability
- Electronic-waste impacts
- Environmental benefits of reuse
- Existing community technology programs
- Published intervention research

---

## September 20, 2026 — Evidence Standards

The following standards will be used throughout the project:

- Correlation will not be described as causation.
- Original analysis will be distinguished from published findings.
- Observed outcomes will be distinguished from modeled scenarios.
- Government, academic, and institutional sources will be preferred where practical.
- Dataset limitations will be documented.
- Conflicting or unfavorable evidence will not be excluded simply because it weakens the proposed intervention.
- Comparable programs will be treated as evidence of feasibility, not proof that identical outcomes would occur elsewhere.
- Recommendations will reflect the strength and limitations of the available evidence.

---

## September 20, 2026 — Proposed Intervention Framing

**Decision:** Treat the proposed nonprofit or community initiative as a potential intervention to be evaluated rather than as a predetermined solution.

A future pilot could potentially include:

- Donated-device collection
- Hardware assessment and refurbishment
- Youth technical and repair training
- Device redistribution
- Digital-literacy education
- Older-adult technical support
- Fraud-awareness education
- Community repair clinics

The capstone will determine which of these components are most strongly supported by the evidence.
