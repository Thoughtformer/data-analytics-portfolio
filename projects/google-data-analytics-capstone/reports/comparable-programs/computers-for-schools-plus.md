# Comparable Program: Computers for Schools Plus

## Program

Computers for Schools Plus (CFS+) is a Canadian program that collects donated computer equipment and electronic devices, refurbishes them, and distributes them to schools, libraries, nonprofit organizations, Indigenous communities, eligible low-income Canadians, and other recipients.

The related Computers for Schools Intern (CFSI) program provides employment internships to youth ages 15–30. Youth interns form an important part of the workforce used in CFS+ refurbishment workshops.

## Why This Program Is Relevant

The program closely resembles several components of the community technology model examined in this capstone:

- collection of donated or surplus technology
- device refurbishment and reuse
- distribution of technology to people with limited access
- hands-on technical training for youth
- development of employment skills
- reduction of electronic waste

It therefore provides evidence that these activities can operate as components of an integrated program rather than as completely separate interventions.

## Evaluation Period

A Government of Canada evaluation examined the CFS+ and CFSI programs from April 2016 through March 2022.

The evaluation used multiple lines of evidence, including:

- program and administrative data
- document and literature review
- stakeholder interviews
- surveys of current and former interns
- case studies

## Device Refurbishment and Distribution

During the evaluation period:

- more than 475,000 refurbished devices were distributed
- the program collected and refurbished donated computer equipment and electronic devices
- equipment that could not be refurbished was directed toward responsible disposal or recycling

The evaluation concluded that the program contributed to increased access to computer technology and reduced the environmental footprint associated with surplus electronic equipment.

## Youth Technical Training

The CFSI program employed 1,674 youth interns during the evaluation period, averaging approximately 279 interns per year.

Reported skills obtained by surveyed interns included:

### Digital Skills

- Hardware repair and refurbishment: 96%
- Data management: 48%
- Development of communication strategies: 29%

### Career Skills

- Interpersonal and teamwork skills: 92%
- Problem solving: 86%
- Organizational skills: 82%

The evaluation found that the internship program provided youth with practical technical experience as well as broader workplace skills.

## Post-Internship Outcomes

Among former interns surveyed:

- 56% were employed following the internship
- 20.5% had returned to school
- 17.8% were seeking employment

Most former interns also reported that the program had at least some influence on their career path.

These results are descriptive program outcomes. They should not be interpreted as proof that participation in the program caused subsequent employment or educational outcomes.

## Program Integration

One of the most important findings for this capstone is the relationship between the refurbishment and youth-training components.

The evaluation reported that youth interns performed much of the refurbishment work conducted by program affiliates.

This demonstrates a practical operating model in which:

surplus technology
→ youth technical training and labor
→ refurbishment
→ device redistribution
→ increased technology access

The same activity therefore has the potential to contribute simultaneously to workforce development, technology access, and electronic reuse.

## Limitations

The program operates in Canada and should not be treated as direct evidence of likely outcomes for a Texas or United States program.

The evaluation also identified inconsistencies and gaps in some program reporting, particularly regarding donor contributions and socioeconomic characteristics of device recipients.

Many findings rely on program records, participant surveys, interviews, and case studies rather than experimental evidence.

The program demonstrates feasibility and observed outcomes, not guaranteed replication.

## Capstone Relevance

The CFS+ and CFSI evaluation provides a real-world precedent for combining:

- donated-device recovery
- refurbishment
- youth technical training
- digital access
- environmental reuse

This strengthens the feasibility argument for an integrated community technology initiative.

It complements the capstone's original analyses:

- NTIA data establishes measurable digital-access and digital-engagement disparities.
- Open Repair data demonstrates substantial repair potential among technology devices presented for community repair.
- CFS+ demonstrates that device refurbishment and youth technical training have been integrated at meaningful operational scale in an existing public program.
