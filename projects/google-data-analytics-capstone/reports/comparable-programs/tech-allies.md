# Comparable Program: Tech Allies

## Program

Tech Allies was a community-based technology-support program designed for socially isolated older adults.

The intervention combined:

- tablet access
- broadband internet access
- one-on-one technology training
- volunteer-based instruction
- in-home support

Participants received eight weekly in-home iPad lessons from trained volunteers.

## Why This Program Is Relevant

The program closely resembles the older-adult support component considered in this capstone.

It demonstrates a model in which access to technology is combined with personalized instruction rather than assuming that providing a device alone is sufficient.

Relevant program elements include:

- device access
- internet access
- individualized technical support
- repeated instruction
- community volunteers
- support for older adults with limited technology experience

## Study Design

The Tech Allies program was evaluated using a pragmatic randomized pilot trial.

Participants were assigned to:

- intervention group: 44 participants
- waitlist comparison group: 39 participants

The study measured:

- technology use
- technology confidence
- loneliness
- social support

Researchers also conducted in-home qualitative interviews with a subgroup of participants.

## Findings

The intervention group showed a statistically significant increase in technology use.

The study also reported marginally significant improvement in:

- technology confidence
- social support

No significant improvement in loneliness was observed.

Qualitative interviews found that some participants reported:

- greater confidence using technology
- feeling more connected to the broader world

The waitlist comparison group did not show similar changes over the study period.

## Interpretation

The study provides evidence that repeated, one-on-one technology instruction can increase technology use among older adults.

The results also suggest that improving digital access or technology skills does not automatically produce broader social outcomes such as reduced loneliness.

This distinction is important for the capstone.

A community technology program can reasonably target measurable outcomes such as:

- increased ability to use devices
- greater technology confidence
- increased use of online tools

Broader outcomes such as reduced social isolation should not be assumed without additional evidence.

## Program Design Relevance

Tech Allies supports a model in which technology access and human assistance are delivered together.

The intervention used:

device + connectivity + personalized instruction

rather than:

device alone

This is relevant to the proposed community technology initiative because older residents may require ongoing, individualized help with tasks such as:

- device setup
- account creation
- communication tools
- internet navigation
- accessing online services
- troubleshooting
- basic digital literacy

The study also demonstrates that community volunteers can participate directly in delivering this support.

## Limitations

This was a relatively small pilot study.

The intervention included 83 participants across the intervention and waitlist groups.

Participants were socially isolated older adults, so the findings should not automatically be generalized to all older adults.

Some outcomes were self-reported.

The study found no significant reduction in loneliness, and improvements in technology confidence and social support were only marginally significant.

The strongest quantitative result was increased technology use.

The study therefore supports the feasibility and potential value of personalized digital training but does not establish that such programs reliably improve all social or well-being outcomes.

## Capstone Relevance

Tech Allies strengthens the evidence for including personalized digital-support services in the proposed community technology model.

Combined with the other capstone evidence:

- NTIA data shows lower technology use and digital engagement among many older adults, particularly those with lower incomes.
- Open Repair data shows that many digital-access devices retain repair potential.
- Computers for Schools Plus demonstrates a model combining refurbishment with youth technical training.
- Tech Allies demonstrates that older adults can receive devices, connectivity, and repeated one-on-one technology instruction through a community-based program.

Together, these sources support treating digital access as more than simply distributing hardware.
